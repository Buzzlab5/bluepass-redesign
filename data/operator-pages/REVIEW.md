Everything is waiting at `/admin/operator-pages`; nothing is public until it is approved
there. Each trip's own check notes say what the researcher was unsure of, and the check
screen flags dates that have passed by itself. This file is the rest: the calls a person
has to make.

## In-water with animals: the permits

42 trips put guests in the water with whales, whale sharks, dolphins, seals or sea lions,
and in every state that is allowed only under a permit or licence. What each site states:

- **Queensland humpback swims, no permit stated on any of them**: Aqua Adventures, Cooly Eco
  Adventures, Whale Swim Safaris (Salty Safaris), Sunreef, Tasman Venture, Whale Swim
  Queensland. Two of the six say only that Queensland allows the swim for licensed operators.
  Confirm each licence before a whale swim is published.
- **New South Wales**: Jervis Bay Wild's permit for in-water humpback encounters comes from
  its own blog post of 11 September 2026, not checked with the authority. Imagine Cruises
  calls itself the only permitted wild dolphin swim in New South Wales; that claim should not
  go out as it stands.
- **Western Australia whale sharks**: Kings Ningaloo gives licences L002014WS and L002015HW,
  Ningaloo Blue Dive gives HQ67531WS, and Three Islands names DBCA. Ningaloo Discovery,
  Ningaloo Marine Interactions, Ningaloo Whaleshark Swim and Coastal Adventure Tours (Coral
  Bay) state nothing.
- **Victoria and South Australia, seals, sea lions and dolphins**: Kangaroo Island Marine
  Adventures says it is licensed to swim with wild dolphins and Temptation Sailing names a
  licence; Moonraker Dolphin Swims and Sea All Dolphin Swims state nothing.

## Certification claims

41 of the 70 pages carry an eco-certification or accreditation claim in their standouts
(Advanced Ecotourism, ECO Certified, Climate Action, Quality Tourism, Sustainable Tourism
Accredited). Every one was read off the operator's own site or a logo and none has been
checked with the certifier. Ecotourism Australia publishes its directory, so the Advanced
and ECO claims are checkable. Three to look at first: BayPlay (awards text reads dated),
Octopus Garden (a Quality Tourism badge with no number or year) and Exmouth Adventure Co
(a Sustainable Tourism Accredited logo that names no accrediting body).

## It is November

The pages were built in mid October. Whale seasons on both coasts have since closed, so
check seasons before approving: Absolute Ocean Charters' whale watching trips were already
at the end of their season when the site was read, and its snubfin dolphin season is stated
three different ways on three pages. In South Australia the harmful algal bloom is the reason
Experiencing Marine Sanctuaries is shutting down, and The Big Duck's page saying its tours
are unaffected dates from 2025.

## No price on the page

182 of the 505 trips carry no price, because the operator shows prices only inside its
booking widget and the research rules do not open those. Eleven operators have no price on
any trip, so their pages would go public with none:

BayPlay (14 trips), Jervis Bay Kayaks (7), Coastal Adventure Tours (6), Kangaroo Island
Ocean Safari (6), Montague Island Adventures (6), Experience Coffin Bay (4), Marine
Adventures Lord Howe (3), Taylor Made Escapes (3), Wildside Adventures (2), Ningaloo Blue
Dive (1), Whitehaven Xpress (1).

Three more are nearly bare: Sunreef (18 of 27), Ningaloo Discovery (14 of 15) and Adelaide
Scuba (11 of 13). Decide whether a page may go public with no price, or waits until the
operator claims it and connects Rezdy.

## Facts that disagree with themselves

65 trips have a fact that the operator's own site states two ways: price, season, duration,
capacity, minimum age, departure time. Each one names both sources and says which was used,
in that trip's check notes. They are worth reading before approving a trip rather than after,
because the correction is kept apart from the website's facts and no later import undoes it.
